The Case Files

What the resume can't say
Payments · reisetopia

Owning the money path

Wherein subscription billing fails in every creative way it can.

Renewals that half-complete. Webhooks that arrive twice — or never. Memberships drifting out of sync with what customers actually paid. I integrated Chargebee and took the entire webhook lifecycle: renewals, failures, cancellations, state sync — so what the customer sees always matches what finance sees.

Outcome — billing that survives its edge cases, not just its happy path.
Event-driven · reisetopia

Rewards as a state machine

A booking becomes loyalty points — through a minefield.

The booking-to-points journey is full of retries, replays and race conditions, each an invitation to award someone twice. I modelled the lifecycle as an explicit state machine with idempotent third-party crediting — duplicates became impossible by construction.

Outcome — zero duplicate awards, by design rather than luck.
Observability · reisetopia

Seeing in the dark

You cannot fix what you cannot see.

I instrumented the platform with Datadog for monitoring and logging and PostHog for product events, then built ETL pipelines aggregating affiliate lead & sales data into BigQuery for the finance product — turning "we think" into "we know" across two departments.

Outcome — failures surface themselves; decisions run on data.
The team · reisetopia

Rebuilding how we hire

The interview process is a product, too.

The process I inherited didn't predict job performance. I redesigned it end to end — reworked the coding challenge, added a system-design round, restructured the interviews into focused stages — then sat in those interviews and helped shape the hires.

Outcome — a process that selects for the work, not for interview tricks.

Op-Ed: How I Work

Instrument everything.

If it isn't monitored, it isn't done. Failures should announce themselves before users do.

Own the gnarly parts.

Payments, retries, race conditions — the unglamorous fifth of the work where products actually break.

Boring over clever.

Code is read at 3 a.m. by tired people. State machines beat spaghetti; explicit beats magic.

Raise the bar.

Standards, architecture reviews, better hiring — leverage compounds through a team.
